1. Where AI Was Used

We used AI to develop the Kotlin Android prototype, investigate gaze-tracking approaches, implement eye-control interactions, and draft documentation. The latest work combines MediaPipe landmarks, estimated camera-to-eye distance, head pose, supervised regression, and pointer smoothing. Evidence includes DepthModel.kt:43, DepthTraining.kt:13, DepthGeometry.kt:37, and InteractionEngine.kt:13 under app/src/main/java/dev/gazedot/.

Human responsibilities included selecting the project, specifying gestures, installing the APK, and reporting observed problems. AI was not used to manufacture participant measurements or real-device accuracy results. The local correction path uses regression rather than an LLM. These are scope boundaries, not a claim that we independently implemented an AI-free subsystem.

3. What AI Did Well

AI implemented an inspectable development workflow: collect target-labelled samples, train two correction models, and evaluate both on a separate session. DepthTraining.kt:13 keeps the final completed session out of training and reports error and coverage. This makes the proposed benefit of distance testable rather than assumed.

It also implemented explicit interaction timing and synthetic numerical checks. Evidence includes app/src/test/java/dev/gazedot/DepthModelTest.kt:7 and InteractionEngineTest.kt:6. The implementation pass recorded a successful APK build, 32 passing unit tests, and lint results of zero errors and 30 warnings. These results support buildability and tested logic, not gaze accuracy. We did not measure time saved.

4. Errors and Overclaims

The clearest failure was treating implementation progress as readiness for use before the model dependency and training requirements were made clear. The user discovered the gap by installing the APK and reporting a missing-model problem and poor horizontal tracking. The conversation does not preserve enough earlier assistant output to quote a specific false assertion; we therefore report the observed failure rather than inventing one.

The correction was to distinguish the bundled pretrained MediaPipe landmark detector from the personalized gaze-correction weights, which still require collected data. The cost was repeated installation, feedback, and rework; elapsed time was not logged. A successful build did not establish that the intended interaction worked on the phone.
